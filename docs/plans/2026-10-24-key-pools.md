## Owner summary (one page)

**What it is.** A *key pool* groups two or more of your existing API-key Connections (same vendor and runtime) behind one name. Asked for a key, the pool hands out the next member that can still serve, in a fixed order that survives restarts. A member the provider refuses for lack of credit (or at a spend limit you set) is marked *exhausted*, skipped, shown to you, and probed again once a day with a one-token call in case you topped it up. Nothing changes for a single key: it stays a Connection and a pool of one behaves as today.

**Why it fits.** A pool member *is* a Connection, so the lease gate (terms, budget, expiry, repo list, isolation) still runs on every key. The pool only decides *which* Connection a request goes to, and it holds no secret: it holds Connection names and health.

**What no API tells us.** Nothing reports a key's balance (your premise; I found no balance endpoint in the pages I read, and did not search for one). So "until empty" means: use a key, learn it is empty from the first refusal, skip it from then on. One request per exhausted key is wasted, and a daily probe costs a fraction of a cent.

**Two helper paths, two behaviours (corrected in revision 2).**
- `morndo-keys lease` (what a hand-written `apiKeyHelper` calls) is **one lease per call**: every call picks again, so rotation and the skip of a reported-dry key take effect at the next call, at most one cache lifetime later.
- `morndo-keys run` starts Claude Code with `helper --lease <id>`, which prints the key of **the one Connection that lease was taken on** (`apps/connections/src/cli.ts:447-465`). A pick made at launch would hold for the whole session, and a dry key would be served until the session ends. So for a pooled `run` the helper becomes **pool-aware** (3.3): each call checks the leased member and, if it is exhausted, closes that lease and takes a freshly gated lease on the next servable member. With the 60 s cache lifetime the launcher already sets (`HELPER_TTL_MS`), a reported dry key costs at most about 60 s of failing calls.
- Claude Code caches helper output five minutes by default and re-runs the helper on a 401 or 403 ([settings reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings-reference.md), `apiKeyHelper`, read 2026-10-24). A credit refusal is not a 401 or 403, so the dry key must be **reported back** (3.4); the clean fix, a local gateway that sees every response, stays deferred.

**Rotation never works around a ban (new, from the review of terms).** The Usage Policy forbids circumventing a ban with another account (section 7, the reviewer's reading; you read the primary text). So the pool rotates automatically **only** on exhausted credit or a spend limit you set. A 401, 403, `account_on_hold`, `authentication_failed` or the tier's monthly cap **stops the pool**, alerts you, and waits for you. A rate limit (429) is not a reason to switch account either.

**Cost (lane-days, one lane):** 11.5 days in five slices (section 8). **Slice 1 (5 days)** is the domain, store, CLI, `lease --pool` and `run --pool`, the Anthropic classifier, and the `run` wrapper's report-back with one retry on the next key. The helper hand-off is slice 2.

**Decisions for you (recommended answer first)** are in section 9, in order. The ones that matter most: the default mode; what a failing member does to the pool (stop, recommended); and the check you owe on Anthropic's Consumer Terms.

## 1. Domain model

### 1.1 Where it lives

A new aggregate **KeyPool** inside the **Connections** context, in `packages/domain/src/key-pool.ts` (pure, the clock is the `now` argument, as `pool.ts` and `connections.ts`). Not a new context: its members are Connections and the gate that serves them is CN8. Not inside `Connection`: a Connection must not know about its siblings (CN15), and a pool needs its own consistency boundary (the cursor, the grants, the probe claims).

**Naming.** `docs/domain.md` already has **Pool** (ADR-0029): the *subscription* accounts of a runtime, with Members, Marks and Seats. The new thing is the **Key pool**, always written with "key" in prose and `KeyPool` / `KeyMember` in code, so a grep never mixes them. They share nothing but the idea of rotation, and their rules differ. A key is never a Pool member (PL1); a subscription is never a Key member (KP2).

### 1.2 Value objects

| Value object | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `CredentialRef` | the **name of a Connection** (matches `CONNECTION_NAME`) | An opaque handle. Not the vault ref (`s-…`), not the fingerprint, never bytes. CN1 and CN5 hold for the pool by construction: it has no field a secret could travel in. |
| `RotationPolicy` | `{ mode: "round-robin" \| "drain", recheckEveryMs }` | `round-robin`: each pick goes to the next servable member after the cursor. `drain` (the owner's "until empty"): stay on the cursor's member while it is servable, move on only when it is not. Defaults: `round-robin`, 24 h. `recheckEveryMs` between 1 h and 7 d (constants of the domain). There is no setting for what a failing member does: it always stops the pool (KP3). A `skip` option was in revision 2 and is removed; if wanted later it may cover `rejected` (a bad key) only, never `denied`, `held` or `cap`, and needs its own decision. |
| `KeyHealth` | `{ state: "active" } \| { state: "exhausted", reason: "credit" \| "spend-limit", resumeAt: number \| null } \| { state: "failing", reason: "rejected" \| "denied" \| "held" \| "cap" }` | Plus per member `since`, `lastChange` and, for exhausted, `nextCheck` and `probingUntil` (below). `rejected` = the provider says the key is bad (401); `denied` = 403; `held` = the account is on hold or its authentication failed; `cap` = the tier's monthly cap. No provider text, no status code. |
| `Outcome` | `ok \| exhausted(reason, resumeAt \| null) \| failing(reason) \| limited(retryAfterMs \| null) \| unknown` | What a *provider adapter* returns (3.1); the only provider-shaped thing that crosses into the domain, already translated. `limited` (a 429 for rate) is **recorded, not acted on**: no skip, no switch (section 7). `unknown` (a 5xx, an overload, a timeout, a malformed request) changes nothing. |
| `Servable` | derived: the member's health is `active` (and its Connection is not revoked) | An exhausted or failing member is never servable; what a failing member does to the pool is KP3. |

### 1.3 Aggregate

**KeyPool** (one per name; the name shares the Connection namespace, KP1):

```
KeyPool {
  name, runtime, vendor,                 // fixed at creation (KP2)
  policy: RotationPolicy,
  members: KeyMember[],                  // ordered; the order is the ring
  cursor: CredentialRef | null,          // the member served last
  grants: Grant[],                       // pooled `run` sessions: who holds which member (3.3)
  sinks: string[],                       // registered env-file paths (3.6), for the lane deny list
  emptyNoticed: boolean,                 // PoolEmpty raised and not yet cleared
  events: KeyPoolEvent[], seq
}
KeyMember { credential, joined, health, since, lastChange, nextCheck | null, probingUntil | null }
Grant { id, member, lease, holder, request, window, previous }   // `request` = role, entry, workspace, limit, reserve, margin: what CN8 needs to run again; `window` = {calls, windowStart} of the helper cap, written on each helper call; `previous` = {member, movedAt} | null (KP18)
```

Persisted as one JSON body with a version, saved only on the version it was read at (the `PoolStore` pattern, `apps/connections/src/adapters/pool-store-sqlite.ts`). A deleted pool leaves a tombstone row so its name is never reused (KP1).

### 1.4 Invariants

| # | Invariant |
|---|---|
| KP1 | A pool name matches `[a-z][a-z0-9-]*` and is unique across pools **and** Connections, revoked ones included (the directory keeps revoked Connections, CN2), and across deleted pools (tombstones). `--connection` and `--pool` can never name the same thing. |
| KP2 | A pool has one vendor and one runtime. A member is a Connection of kind `api-key`, custody `morndo`, status not `revoked`, with that vendor and runtime. A Connection is in at most one key pool. A subscription, an engine-layer Connection, or a key of another vendor is refused at join. |
| KP3 | **A member is never served while exhausted.** `pick` considers only servable members: `active`. A `failing` member is not servable, and it **blocks the pool**: every pick refuses with `pool_blocked` until the owner revives or removes that member, so traffic never moves to another account because one account was refused. There is no skipping option in v1. |
| KP4 | **Rotation order is deterministic and survives restarts.** The order is the member list (join order). `round-robin`: the pick is the first servable member after `cursor` in ring order, wrapping; with no cursor, the first. `drain`: `cursor` while servable, else the next servable after it. The cursor is saved with the pick, before the pick is returned. Same state in, same pick out; no randomness, no clock in the choice. |
| KP5 | A pick that finds no servable member returns `pool_empty` (with the earliest `nextCheck` or `resumeAt`) or `pool_blocked`, and serves nothing. It never falls back to an exhausted or failing member. |
| KP6 | An outcome carries `observedAt`. One older than the member's `lastChange` (any transition, including a recovery) changes nothing, so a request that started before a mark or a recovery cannot undo it. |
| KP7 | `exhausted` is left only by `ok` from a **real Messages request** (a probe or a consumer report; `KeyRecovered`) or by the owner's `revive`. `failing` is left only by the owner's `revive`, or by the Connection's rotation to a new key (`ConnectionRotated`), never by a probe: a refused account is not retried behind the owner's back. Evidence from `count_tokens`, `/v1/models` or any call that is not a Messages request is never `ok` (3.2). |
| KP8 | Health is per pool member, not per Connection. Removing a member discards its health and its grants. Revoking a Connection ends its membership in the same step. |
| KP9 | A pool grants nothing and gates nothing (CN15). The lease gate (CN8) runs on the member the pick names. If it refuses (`off`, `terms`, `expired`, `repo`, `isolation`, `budget`), the service tries the next servable member in ring order, at most `members.length` times; **the cursor moves to the member finally leased** (so `drain` does not return to a declined member on the next pick), exactly one `MemberPicked` is written per pick, carrying the declined members' gate words. A gate refusal never changes health. If every member is declined, the refusal is the first member's gate word, exit 75. |
| KP10 | Narrowing is anyone's: removing a member, switching a member off (CN14), setting `drain`. Widening is the principal's, judged by effect: creating a pool, adding a member (it may now serve), `revive`, and acknowledging the pool's terms line. |
| KP11 | `PoolEmpty` and `PoolBlocked` are each raised once on the transition and not again until a member is servable again; `KeyExhausted` and `KeyFailed` once per transition (a repeat refusal with an older `observedAt` raises nothing). |
| KP12 | Events carry names, reason words, times and the command id. They never carry the key, a fingerprint, a vault ref, a provider message, a hook's `last_assistant_message` or `error_details`, a status code, a header or a request id. |
| KP13 | Opt-in per provider. Nothing creates a pool by itself; a Connection with no pool is served by `lease --connection` exactly as today. A pool of one member serves that member and is, for every output, the single Connection's behaviour plus health. |
| KP14 | Changing the order of members is the principal's (`move`), since it changes which key is spent first. It does not move the cursor's member. |
| KP15 | **Concurrent picks are serialised by the pool's version.** Two concurrent picks in `round-robin` get different members when two are servable (the second sees the first's cursor); in `drain` both get the same member. A lost save is retried (up to 8) on fresh state; the cursor is never advanced twice for one pick. |
| KP16 | **A probe is claimed before it is made.** Starting a probe sets the member's `probingUntil = now + 30 s` and `nextCheck` in the same saved write; a caller that finds `probingUntil` in the future does not probe. N sessions hitting an empty pool cause one probe, not N; a probe that dies leaves the claim to lapse. |
| KP17 | **A hand-off keeps the helper window.** When a pooled helper call replaces a lease (3.3), the new lease inherits the old one's call count and window start, so the per-window cap (CN9) cannot be reset by moving to another member. |
| KP18 | **A report right after a hand-off is attributed to the old member.** A grant keeps `previous = {member, movedAt}`. A report made through the grant (the `run` wrapper, `--from-hook --grant`) with `observedAt < movedAt + HELPER_TTL` is attributed to `previous.member`, because Claude Code may still have been using the old cached key (an in-flight request, a second caller). Because that could hide a dry new key, such a report also claims one probe (KP16) of the current member, which decides it. After the window `previous` is cleared and a report is the current member's. A report that names a member (`write-env`, manual) is never re-attributed. |
| KP19 | **The directory is the truth for leases; `handOff` is recoverable.** A lease swap (close the old, grant the new) is one directory save; the grant is saved after it. A crash between the two leaves a live lease of the grant's holder on a pool member that the grant does not name; the next call **adopts** it (3.3, table). No call ever leaves two live leases for one grant, and none writes a second `KeyServed` for a key it did not print. |

### 1.5 Domain events

`KeyPoolCreated`, `KeyPoolRemoved`, `MemberJoined`, `MemberLeft`, `PolicyChanged`, `MemberPicked` (pool, member, at, declined members' gate words; written with the cursor), `GrantOpened`, `GrantMoved` (from member, to member, why), `GrantClosed`, `KeyExhausted` (member, reason, resumeAt), `KeyFailed` (member, reason), `KeyLimited` (member, retry-after seconds; informational), `KeyRecovered` (member, how: `probe` \| `report` \| `revive`), `PoolEmpty` (backAt), `PoolBlocked` (member, reason), `PoolRefilled`, `ProbeClaimed`. Widening events carry the command id (as CN13).

`LeaseGranted`, `KeyServed`, `LeaseClosed` stay Connection events and are unchanged: the audit of a served key is the Connection's; the audit of *why that key* is the pool's `MemberPicked` / `GrantMoved`.

## 2. Application services and ports

New file `apps/connections/src/application/key-pool-service.ts`, the same shape as `PoolService` (load, apply one domain function, save on the version read, up to 8 retries). It imports only the domain, the protocol and its siblings (`apps/connections/test/architecture.test.ts` enforces that; `no-vendor-names.test.ts` forbids any preset id or label in it).

| Service call | Does |
|---|---|
| `create / join / leave / move / setPolicy / revive / remove` | The commands of 3.5, with KP1, KP2 and KP10 checks. |
| **`lease(pool, request)`** → `{ member, leaseId }` or a refusal | Pick (KP4, KP9, cursor saved, `MemberPicked`), then `LeaseService.lease(member, request)`. On PoolEmpty with a member due: **claim** the probe (KP16), probe once (5 s bound), pick again. Refusals carry distinct words on stderr: `pool_empty`, `pool_blocked`, or the gate's word (`terms`, `repo`, `budget`, …); all exit 75 as the engine layer treats 75 as "provider unavailable", and the engine layer's mark reason is the word, so "all keys dry" and "not allowed here" read apart. |
| **`handOff(pool, grant, caller)`** | The pooled helper's call (3.3). |
| **`report(pool \| member, outcome, observedAt)`** | KP6, KP7; raises the Alerts. The one door every consumer uses. |
| `recheck(now)` | For each exhausted member with `nextCheck <= now` and no live `probingUntil`: claim, probe, `report`. At most one probe per member per call. |
| `state(pool)` | Members in ring order with health, reason word, since, nextCheck, last pick; no ref, no fingerprint. |
| `onRevoked(connection)` | Removes the member (KP8); called by `ConnectionsService.revoke` in the same retry loop. |

**Alerts** use the existing `AlertSink`: `{kind: "key-exhausted" | "key-failing" | "pool-empty" | "pool-blocked" | "unclassified", name, detail}`; `detail` is a reason word. `page.ts` maps kinds to words; five words are added to `CONNECTION_WORDS`.

**Ports** (all in `application/ports.ts`; no port returns a secret):

```ts
type KeyPoolStore = {                       // the PoolStore pattern: one row per pool, JSON body, version
  load(name: string): { pool: KeyPool; version: number };
  names(): readonly string[];
  save(pool: KeyPool, version: number): boolean;   // false when `version` is no longer current
};
type ProviderProbe = {                      // adapter-side; it opens the vault itself, the application never sees bytes
  probe(member: { connection: string; vendor: string }): Promise<Outcome>;
```

## 3. Adapters

### 3.1 Provider adapter (Anthropic first): classify

`apps/connections/src/adapters/outcome-anthropic.ts`. **What the docs say** (read 2026-10-24):

| Signal | Source | Domain outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 2xx on a Messages request | | `ok` |
| 400 `invalid_request_error` whose message says the credit balance is too low | The API message is documented: "Your credit balance is too low to access the Anthropic API. Please go to Plans & Billing to upgrade or purchase credits." Claude Code prints a shorter "Credit balance too low · Add funds: https://platform.claude.com/settings/billing" ([API credits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/api-credits-for-subscribers)). Status and error type are **not documented**; 400 is the owner's observation | `exhausted(credit)` |
| 402 `billing_error` ("an issue with your billing or payment information") | [errors](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/errors) | `exhausted(credit)` |
| 400 `invalid_request_error` whose message starts "You have reached your specified API usage limits" (or "…workspace API usage limits"): **this 400 is also what an owner-set spend limit returns** | [rate limits](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/rate-limits) | `exhausted(spend-limit, resumeAt from the message date when present)` |
| 429 `rate_limit_error` with `error.details.error_code` = `enforced_spend_limit_reached`, no `retry-after` (the tier's monthly cap; access resumes 00:00 UTC on the 1st) | same | `failing(cap)`: a limit Anthropic set, not the owner |
| 429 `rate_limit_error` otherwise | same | `limited(retry-after)`: recorded, no skip |
| 401 `authentication_error` | errors | `failing(rejected)` |
| 403 `permission_error` | errors | `failing(denied)` |
| the Claude Code workspace limit 429 | rate limits | `limited` |
| any other 400, a 404, 413, 5xx, 529, 504, a network error | | `unknown`. **A malformed request must never exhaust a key.** |

Rules for the classifier:
1. Match the two 400 cases on the message text, case-insensitively; accept either of the two strings (the API body and Claude Code's shorter line), since the `run` wrapper and `keypool watch` read what Claude prints. The docs say error `type` values may expand, so the classifier is fixture-driven (section 8), and an unmatched 4xx raises an `unclassified` Alert carrying the status and error type only, never the text.
2. Keys of one organization share its limits (the docs say limits are organization-level). Two members of one organization gain nothing against a 429 or a monthly cap; the page warns at join if the `anthropic-organization-id` response header (documented in the errors page) shows two members share one. The id is kept as an opaque string, only for that warning.
3. The classifier receives status, error type, a bounded message (256 characters), `retry-after` and the date in the message; it matches, returns an `Outcome`, and drops them.

### 3.2 The probe, and what may count as recovery

One `POST /v1/messages` to the preset's smallest listed model (the existing `smallModel` helper), `max_tokens: 1`, one short user message. About 10 input tokens and 1 output token; the catalog's dated prices give the figure per probe, and the day's total is shown. It is recorded as a Connection `SpendRecorded` estimate so budgets stay honest.

**Only a real Messages request is evidence of recovery (KP7).** Token counting is documented as free to use with its own separate rate limits ([token counting](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/token-counting), as the reviewer read it), so a dry key may well answer 200 there, which would falsely recover the member. `/v1/models` is not documented either way for credit. Neither is ever a probe. The earlier spike S1 is dropped; no test or probe uses them. (One use stays harmless and optional: asking the owner's known-empty key whether `/v1/models` lists models, to explain a confusing symptom, never as health.)

The recheck's `nextCheck` is `since + recheckEveryMs`, or `resumeAt` when the refusal named a date (a spend limit that resumes on the 1st is not probed daily until then); after a failed probe the interval doubles to a cap of 7 days.

### 3.3 Consumer adapter A: Claude Code `apiKeyHelper`

**Form 1, `morndo-keys lease --pool P`** (the hand-written helper): one lease per call. The service picks (KP4, KP9) and the existing `leaseKey` body runs for the member (caller proof, isolation, `KeyServed`, close). A dry key reported back is skipped at the next call; with Claude Code's five-minute default cache, at most one cache lifetime later. `--pool` and `--connection` are exclusive; `--member` is refused (the caller picks a pool, never a key).

**Form 2, `morndo-keys run --pool P`** (the launcher): today `run` takes one lease and the child's helper is `helper --lease <id>`, bound to that lease and so to one Connection. For a pooled run:
- `run --pool` opens a **Grant** in the pool (member, lease, holder, the request parameters CN8 needs) in the same step as the first lease, and the child's helper line becomes `helper --pool P --grant <id>`. A single-Connection `run` is untouched.
- **Each helper call** (`handOff`) first checks the caller (the grant holder or its descendant, the CN9 ancestry check) and then reads the grant, its lease in the directory and its member's health. The cases, in this order:

  | State found | Action |
  |---|---|
  | lease live, authority live, member `active`, refresh passes the gate | serve through the normal path (refresh, count the call in `grant.window`, `KeyServed`, print) |
  | lease live, member `exhausted` | **swap**: pick the next servable member (KP4, KP9) and gate it with the grant's stored request (**CN8 runs again**) |
  | lease live, member `active`, but the refresh is refused with a gate word (`budget`, `expired`, `off`, `terms`) | **swap** as above; health is untouched (KP9) |
  | lease live, member `failing`, or the pool blocked | print nothing, exit 1 with `pool_blocked` (KP3) |
  | lease live, but its authority has **ended** (the helper cap, CN9) | print nothing, exit 1 with `authority`. No swap and no new lease: a hand-off must not be a way round the cap (KP17) |
  | grant's lease **not live** (closed, lost, swept, or replaced by a crashed swap) and the holder has one live lease on a pool member that the grant does not name | **adopt** that lease: set `grant.lease`, `grant.member` and `previous` from the directory, then serve normally (KP19) |
  | grant's lease not live, no such live lease, member servable | take a new gated lease on the **same** member with the stored request, inheriting `grant.window` (KP17); if the window's cap is already used up, exit 1 with `authority` |
  | grant's lease not live, no such lease, member not servable | pick and gate the next member as in a swap |
  | no member servable, or the gate refuses every candidate | print nothing, exit 1 with `pool_empty` or the gate's word |

  Claude Code then reports "apiKeyHelper script is failing" within three attempts for any exit 1 ([authentication](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/authentication.md), read 2026-10-24).
- **The order of writes in a swap, and what a crash leaves.**
  1. *Directory save (one CAS write):* close the old lease with outcome `handoff` (its reservation is charged as the estimate, the existing close semantics) **and** grant the new lease, in one `store.save`, so the pair is atomic. This needs one pure domain composition (`swapLease`: `closeLease` then `lease`, the new lease inheriting the call count and window start, KP17).
  2. *Pool save:* `grant.member`, `grant.lease`, `grant.window`, `previous = {old member, now}`, `GrantMoved`. If it loses the version, re-read and retry; if the grant has meanwhile moved, this call adopts it (table row 6).
  3. *Serve:* `serveKey` writes `KeyServed` for the new lease **before** the key is printed (unchanged).
  A crash before step 1 changes nothing. A crash between 1 and 2 leaves the old lease closed, a new one live on the new member, and the grant still naming the old: the next call finds "lease not live" plus a live lease of the holder on a pool member and **adopts** it, with no second lease and no second `KeyServed`. A crash between 2 and 3 leaves a consistent grant and a lease with no `KeyServed`; the key was never printed, so "served implies recorded" holds, and the next call serves it. A crash mid-`serve` is today's case (the `KeyServed` line is written first).
- Two helper calls handing off at once are serialised by the **directory's** version (step 1): the loser re-reads, finds the old lease closed and a live lease of the holder on the new member, and adopts it (KP19). Exactly one live lease is left.
- A hand-off happens only when the current member is unservable; a healthy run stays on its key (the round-robin applies per run and per `lease` call, not within a run).
- The run's final cost is reported at its end and goes to the **last** member's lease; earlier members were charged their reservation at hand-off. This is an estimate-level inaccuracy, stated and accepted: budgets are estimates already (ADR-0018, CN10).
- The run's end closes the grant; the sweep removes grants whose holder is gone (as `LeaseService.sweep` does for leases).
- **Attribution after a swap (KP18).** The helper call that swaps returns the new key to its caller, so that caller caches the new key. The old key can still be in use by an in-flight request, or by a second process of the same run that has not asked again yet (up to `HELPER_TTL_MS`). A credit refusal arriving in that window is attributed to `previous.member`, and one claimed probe of the current member confirms it is healthy (or marks it). The `run` wrapper's "last served member" is read from the grant, so it follows the same rule.
- **Bound on loss.** From the moment a report-back marks the member exhausted, the next helper call hands off. The launcher sets `CLAUDE_CODE_API_KEY_HELPER_TTL_MS` to 60 s (`runtime-claude.ts:425`, `HELPER_TTL_MS`), so a report-back bounds the failing time to about 60 s. Before the report-back there is no bound except the detection time (3.4).

### 3.4 How an exhausted key gets reported back

Each path ends in `KeyPoolService.report`:

1. **The `run` wrapper (slice 1).** `morndo-keys run` supervises the child and reads its final failure (a headless `-p` turn prints the API error). It classifies the text with the same table as 3.1 (both strings), reports `exhausted` or `failing` for the member the run was last served, and, for a headless turn, retries **once** on the next servable member. Covers engine-layer lanes.
2. **A `StopFailure` hook for interactive sessions (slice 4).** Documented facts ([hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks.md), as read by the reviewer and in part by me, 2026-10-24): the event runs instead of Stop when the turn ends due to an API error; its input has the common fields plus `error` (one of `rate_limit`, `overloaded`, `authentication_failed`, `oauth_org_not_allowed`, `account_on_hold`, `billing_error`, `invalid_request`, `model_not_found`, `server_error`, `max_output_tokens`, `cloud_credential_error`, `unknown`), optional `error_details`, and optional `last_assistant_message` (the rendered error text); its output and exit code are ignored; it runs in `-p` too. **What is not documented is which `error` a credit refusal receives.** So the spike S2 shrinks to that: map both `billing_error` and `invalid_request` to "look at the text", and classify `last_assistant_message` with the 3.1 table; an unmatched text is `unknown`. The launcher's `--settings` carries the hook: `StopFailure` → `morndo-keys keypool report --from-hook --grant <id>`. **The hook's stdin is read, classified and dropped**: `last_assistant_message` and `error_details` are never logged, stored or put in an event (KP12), and the planted-secret test covers this path (section 8).
3. **The probes (slice 3).** The daily recheck, and the inline probe when the pool is empty. They also find a key that ran dry while nobody ran a lane.

A server consumer (3.6) calls `keypool report` itself.

A deferred fourth path, the **local gateway**, sees every response and reports exactly; it would also give per-request rotation. ADR-0018 deferred it. Not in this plan.

### 3.5 Hub / CLI surface (the owner's opt-in)

`morndo-keys pool …` already means the *account* pool (`apps/connections/src/pool-cli.ts`). The new commands are **`morndo-keys keypool …`**:

```
keypool create <name> --vendor V --runtime R [--mode round-robin|drain] [--recheck 24h] <key-connection>...   # principal; shows the terms line
keypool add <name> <connection>        keypool remove <name> <connection>     keypool move <name> <connection> --to N
keypool policy <name> [--mode …] [--recheck …]
keypool list                           # name, mode, members in ring order, health, reason, since, next check, last pick
keypool show <name>                    # one pool with its recent events (no secrets)
keypool report <name> --member M --outcome exhausted|failing|limited|ok [--from-hook --grant G]
keypool recheck [<name>] [--member M]  # probes the members due (claimed, KP16); --member probes now
keypool revive <name> <connection>     # principal
keypool delete <name>                  # members stay, as plain Connections; the name stays taken
```

Exit codes follow the file (0 done, 1 refused with the reason on stderr, 75 gate refusal or an unavailable pool, with the reason word). `list` and `show` print JSON lines like `list` for Connections. Opt-in is `create`; nothing groups keys that merely share a vendor. The command words go into the one table in `packages/protocol`; `create`, `add`, `revive` need the principal's command id (KP10).

The hub does not run these. It reads state through the same call path as the connections page (`apps/hub/src/adapters/connections-cli.ts`, `execFile`): `keypool list` is added to the page's read; widening commands stay at the machine.

### 3.6 Consumer adapter B: a server that reads one key from a `.env`

For a server that cannot call a helper (an extraction service reading `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` from its `.env`, `extractor` below): `morndo-keys keypool write-env <name> --file <path> --var ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`.

- **One lease per write, not one for the file's life.** The command picks a member and takes a gated lease (probe-style, reserve 0, holder = the writer), writes `KeyServed`, writes the file, and closes the lease. (Revision 1 held a lease "for the file's life"; `LeaseService.sweep` closes leases whose holder process is gone, so that lease would be reaped at once. The audit of exposure is the `KeyServed` at the write.)
- **The value goes to `<path>` as `VAR=value`** through a temp file in the same directory, mode 0600, then `rename`, so a reader never sees a half-written file. It refuses a path inside a git work tree, in a world-readable directory, or under the vault.
- **The path is registered and denied to lanes.** `write-env` first requires `keypool sink add <name> <path>` (principal), which stores the path in the pool's `sinks`. Every sink path is added to the lane deny list the launcher builds (`deny: [home.vaultDir, home.db, home.identity, …]` at `cli.ts:266`, and the same keys in the sandbox settings of 3.3's launcher), so a lane cannot read the key by path. Revision 1's "the pool adds no exposure" was wrong without this: a rotated key in a fresh path is a new place a same-user lane could read. The server's own host access is the owner's, as today.
- The server picks the new value up on its own restart or reload; the writer does not signal it (no process control).
- `write-env --if-due` rewrites when the served member is no longer servable, or when `--rotate-every` has passed (`round-robin` only). Run from a user timer or the server's pre-start.
- **Report-back:** the server (or a wrapper at its start) calls `keypool report <name> --member <m> --outcome exhausted` on the credit refusal. The member name is in a sidecar `<path>.member` (the name only, 0600, also in the deny list). `keypool watch --log <file>` is the option when the server cannot be changed: it matches the classifier table on the log's lines, **never echoes or copies the matched line** (it reports only the outcome word and the member), and its own output passes the exact-value filter. Without either, the daily probe finds it within a day.

### 3.7 The web card

One card on the existing **LLM connections** page (`apps/web/src/lib/connections/ConnectionsBoard.svelte`), read-only in v1: per pool, the mode, members in ring order, a health chip (active / exhausted / failing), the reason word, since, next check, last pick, and the day's probe cost. The page allowlist (`ConnectionsPage`, a strict schema in `packages/protocol/src/connections.ts`) gets a `pools` array of exactly those fields; **no ref, no fingerprint, no secret, no sink path**. Actions stay at the machine in v1. A phone notification for `pool-empty` and `pool-blocked` rides the existing alert path.

## 4. "Until empty" when the provider gives no balance

1. A member starts `active`. Nothing is read from the provider.
2. It serves until a refusal classified `exhausted` arrives (3.4). `KeyExhausted` is raised, the Alert shows on the page and the phone, and the next pick or helper call moves on. The cost is one refused request per exhausted key (a refused request is not billed), plus the detection time.
3. While another member is servable, nothing else happens. When none is, `PoolEmpty` is raised once (KP11), and picks refuse with `pool_empty` and the earliest return time, so the engine layer takes the provider out and falls to the next in the role's chain.
4. **Recheck.** `nextCheck` as in 3.2 (24 h default, or `resumeAt`, doubling to 7 d after a failed probe). The timer is a user systemd unit on the node (`morndo-keys keypool recheck`), plus the claimed inline probe when the pool is empty and a member is due (KP16). A successful Messages probe returns `ok` → `KeyRecovered`; the member rejoins the ring in its place.
5. `drain` is the same with one difference: the cursor stays on a member until it is not servable, so keys are spent one after another.
6. A **failing** member is different from an exhausted one: the pool is blocked (KP3) until the owner acts, and the page and phone say which member and why.
7. The owner sees every transition: page, `keypool list`, Alerts, event log. A key topped up is back within a day, or at once with `keypool recheck --member`.
8. Morndo's own budget (CN8, CN10) is separate: a member may carry a Budget as a second stop; it declines the key (KP9), it does not mark it exhausted.

## 5. Security

- **Secrets stay in the store.** The pool's model, events, logs, Alerts, the page and the board hold names and reason words only (KP12, CN1, CN5). The planted-secret test (section 8) covers the pool JSON, its events, CLI output, the alert file, the page message, **and the `report` / `--from-hook` path** (a hook payload whose `last_assistant_message` and `error_details` carry a planted secret must leave no trace after classification).
- **Leases stay per call.** `lease --pool` is one lease per call, `KeyServed` written before the key is printed, the lease closed after. A pooled `run` holds one lease at a time, replaced by a gated one at hand-off; `write-env` takes one per write. The caller proof, `--isolation`, the pinned `claude` binary and the signed-release check apply to the picked member unchanged (including a floor of `os`).
- **The pick is not a capability.** A caller names a pool, never a key; `lease --pool` refuses `--member`. Only the principal's `create`/`add` decides who can be served.
- **Probes and classification** open the key inside the adapter, send it only to the preset's base URL (`baseUrlProblem` and the first-party-host rule apply), and never log a request or response body; the message string is never persisted.
- **A pool member's secret reaches a server consumer** only through `write-env` (3.6): a 0600 temp file renamed into place, a registered sink path that is on the lanes' deny list, the exact-value filter on the node's output paths, and no printing.
- **Same-user processes are trusted** (the ADR-0018 limit). A process of the same user can read `connections.db` and see pool state; it cannot read keys without the vault identity.

## 6. Migration

- **Single keys keep working untouched.** `lease --connection`, `run --connection`, the engine-layer providers, the Priority and the page all read Connections as before. A Connection in no pool is not different in any output.
- **A pool of one equals today.** `lease --pool solo` serves the one member; the additions are `MemberPicked` and health. A test pins byte-identical stdout and the same `KeyServed`/`LeaseClosed` sequence.
- **Store.** One additive migration (4): table `key_pool (name TEXT PRIMARY KEY, version INTEGER, body TEXT)` in `connections.db` (`store-migrations.ts`, `compat: "additive"`; the schema-floor, downgrade-contract and migrations-additive tests cover it). A node on the previous release opens the database and ignores the table, as with migration 3.
- **Priority and the engine layer (slice 2).** A pool is addressed by `--pool`. The engine layer sees it as a provider named after the pool, with `bin: morndo-keys run --pool P --` and `probe: morndo-keys check --pool P` (the `gateBin` in `engines-sync.ts`). When a Connection joins, its own engine-layer provider is turned off and the pool's provider takes its place in the Priority order; leaving restores it. Both are structural changes, so they re-derive chains (ADR-0018 §4) through the existing projection, with the drift rules unchanged.
- **Existing keys.** Joining does not rotate, re-seal or change a key's fingerprint, budget or expiry.
- **Rollback.** `keypool delete` leaves the members as ordinary Connections and restores their providers; the table is never dropped.

## 9. Owner decisions, with recommended answers

1. **Default mode.** `round-robin` (spreads spend; skips dry keys), with `drain` as the opt-in "one key until empty, then the next". *Recommended: round-robin default, drain available.*
2. **A failing member** (401, 403, account on hold, the tier's monthly cap): the pool blocks and alerts until you act. *Recommended: stop, and no skip option in v1.* It keeps the pool from moving traffic to another account because one account was refused (section 7); a later `skip` for a bad key (`rejected`) only is possible, never for `denied`, `held` or the cap.
3. **Where it lives.** Inside Connections, a new aggregate, commands under `keypool`. *Recommended: yes.*
4. **Build order of report-back.** `run` wrapper (slice 1) → pooled helper hand-off (slice 2) → probes (3) → `StopFailure` hook and `write-env` report (4). *Recommended: this order; ship slice 1 first.*
5. **Gateway.** Defer the local gateway. *Recommended: defer; revisit once the report-back paths show how often a dry key reaches a session.*
6. **Priority entry.** A pool replaces its members in the Priority and chains (members' own providers go off while pooled). *Recommended: yes.*
7. **Recheck.** Daily, doubling to 7 days, or at the date the refusal names; one Messages probe of about a dozen tokens; plus the claimed inline probe when the pool is empty. *Recommended: yes.* My estimate from the catalog's dated prices is well under a cent a day for ten dry keys; slice 3 measures it.

9. **Budget on a member.** Optional second stop from your own accounting. *Recommended: keep optional.*
10. **Server consumer.** `write-env` with a registered sink, one lease per write, plus a report command; the daily probe is the backstop; `watch` only if the server cannot call a command. *Recommended: yes.*
11. **Web actions.** Read-only card in v1; all widening commands stay at the machine. *Recommended: yes.*
