Messaging, now and with the research

One made-up morning for Mara, who owns Partnerships, in six moments: catching up, asking, instructing, changing the goal, deciding, and a teammate finishing work. First as it works today (one Manager chat, topic chats that are a plain group chat), then with eight findings from management and groupware research applied. 04The eight findings

    What we would build

    1. A topic is a group chat for its people and its agents. The Manager is not in it; it reads the shared record.
    2. Agents speak only to deliver or to ask. Status lives on the topic page, never in the chat.
    3. Every message is sorted by the topic's manager (question, request, done, decision, change) and shows a small label the person can correct. Nobody picks a category.
    4. A request becomes a lane thread with an owner and a state, linked from the message; an instruction gets one read-back receipt.
    5. Decisions are pinned and open as a brief in Decide, with the chat's discussion attached.
    6. "Since you last looked" opens every topic chat.
    7. Only the topic's manager speaks outward, and only for what spans topics: goals, priorities, budgets, shared resources. The Manager approves those with you.

    Open: should agents post in the same stream as people, or in a quieter strip of their own? The research on overload points to a strip.
